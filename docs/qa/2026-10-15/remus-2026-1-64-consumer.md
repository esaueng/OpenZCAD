## Demo fillet repair and compatibility

The original seed placed the boss and its bore at `plate_t + wall_h / 2` unconditionally. With `wall_h = 26`, the boss opening is only 2.5 mm below the top edge; with `boss_r = 14`, it is only 1.5 mm below. The requested 3 mm blend crosses that opening. Instrumenting the typed kernel outcome reproduced `cliff-encountered` at exactly those clearances in `reject_blend_into_hole`; these are geometry refusals, not missing edge references. The old package's acceptance is not sufficient evidence of a valid result: the hole/setback guard prevents a contact curve crossing an inner wire. Moving the fillet before the boss also refuses the resulting intersecting curved-face union under the exact-only gate.

The production bracket seed now shares this height expression between the boss and bore:

```text
plate_t + min(wall_h / 2, wall_h - 0.5 - boss_r - fillet_r - 0.25)
```

The 0.5 mm is the existing wall/base overlap; 0.25 mm is intentional design clearance below the fillet contact line, not a kernel tolerance. Default dimensions and default geometry stay unchanged. Where a short wall or large boss needs clearance, the boss and coaxial bore move down together:

| Configuration | Old boss/bore z | Repaired z | Fillet radius |
| ------------- | --------------: | ---------: | ------------: |
| Defaults      |           24 mm |      24 mm |          3 mm |
| `wall_h = 26` |           21 mm |   20.25 mm |          3 mm |
| `boss_r = 14` |           24 mm |   22.25 mm |          3 mm |

This deliberately changes the seed's placement rule for near-top configurations. It does not add general support for a blend running into a boss or hole. On opening an owned, original saved bracket, the application recognizes the deterministic demo ID, all 17 feature recipes and links, original placement expressions and seed/parameter command kinds, then migrates only the two placement fields in current nodes, serialized creation commands and stored history endpoints together. Parameter values, IDs, checkpoints, command metadata, history entry IDs/labels, actor, cursor and pending Redo remain intact; an audit revision records the repair. Shared/viewer documents, custom recipes and user-edited features are left alone. The repair is idempotent, uses the current authenticated session during startup, and keeps Undo/Redo on the user’s parameter edits. User modeling edits—including those on a pending Redo branch—make the recipe ineligible. Arbitrary combinations beyond the demonstrated parameter range can still refuse.

All original 21 demo tests pass without changing their parameter values, radius, warning-free assertions or exportable-body requirements. Three added geometry cases independently require two full-length radius-3 cylindrical bands, the coaxial boss and bore at the positions above, positive lower clearance, exact quarter-cylinder material removal, zero boundary/non-manifold mesh edges, and actual STEP export. Two saved-document regressions first reproduce the old refusal, then require warning-free STEP export, parameter/history preservation, idempotence, cold serialized-command replay and parameter Undo/Redo, including an actor different from the owner. Two further cases preserve pending Redo through repair and actual STEP export. Custom-project/recipe controls abstain. Four browser regressions exercise fresh and previously saved documents through the real worker, durable IndexedDB repair/reload and the unchanged two-edge `fillet_r` editor; both fresh and saved demos exercise their parameter Undo/Redo.
