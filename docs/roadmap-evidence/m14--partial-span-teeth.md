Partial-span teeth (schema v55): "partial span" means teeth cut along part of the cylinder's length, the axial band the old `partial span` refusal turned away; sector gears (teeth over part of the circumference) are out of scope. Teeth on a body take an optional `faceWidth` (their length; absent runs from the offset to the face's far end), `offset` and `anchor` (`start`, the face's lower end along its axis, or `end`), which the adapter turns into Remus `cutGearTeethDetailed`'s axial `offset` and `faceWidth`; the cylinder either side stays as collars. A whole-face gear stores none of them, so it still builds as before and older documents need no migration; an older client would cut a partial span along the whole face, so v55 refuses there. Every face is named: the kernel's tooth roles, `gear.face.collar.start|end` for what is left of the picked face beside the teeth (only coaxial faces on its axis line and within its length, so a same-diameter step elsewhere on the shaft is never taken for a collar; always by role, even where one collar could carry the face's old name, so an offset or length edit that makes one collar two or two one keeps the names and references to them), `gear.face.shoulder.start|end` at the teeth's ends (`shoulder.start[k]` per tooth space, by the root it borders, where keep-diameter tips stand on the collar and split the shoulder), and `gear.face.cap.start|end` for the picked face's cap at either end, as the whole-face cut names it, so a reference to a cap holds as the teeth go from the whole face to part of it and back: at an end the teeth stop short of, the plane there that bounds the collar; at an end they reach, the cap is split, the gear's toothed ring keeps `gear.face.cap.*` and the body's part of it, inside (a boss) or outside (a bore) the ring, is `gear.face.cap.*.body`. Known limitation: a reference to the split-off part (`gear.face.cap.*.body`) does not survive the caps rejoining (teeth back along the whole face, or stopping short of that end). It refuses by name, "The selected part of the gear's end cap no longer exists. Re-pick the face.", and never resolves to the ring or the rejoined cap; a later feature that trims or splits that part refuses the same way. Following it into the rejoined cap needs rejoin provenance, a candidate for the gear edge and face naming follow-up. Other faces the body's lineage already names keep that name. Fillets and chamfers on a gear result's edges are pinned to edge hashes: gear results publish face lineage only, no edge lineage (true of every M14 gear body, not just partial spans), so an edge modifier on a shoulder or collar does not follow a span edit; naming gear edges from their two adjacent face names is a separate app-side follow-up PR, which the owner chose over a Remus change. Partial bands skip cap unification: the kernel's whole-solid unify would also merge each tip land into its collar, which it reverts or leaves unmeasurable, so such an end cap stays two named faces. Teeth past the face refuse as `gear:span-outside-face`; a zero length, a negative offset or an unknown anchor are command refusals, and an offset or anchor on a new gear is refused. A cap drag on partial teeth is no longer read as the source cylinder's height, which would slide the stored span. The Gear teeth tool picks its end from the click, as the Thread does, and shares the Thread's length arrow, Offset row and their refusals (Length and Offset under Options, in the document's length unit); History edits round-trip the span and returning to the whole face clears it.
