ADR-024 conformance, contacts heads in device approval (verified pins): a device approved by another device receives the approver's newest contacts head inside the authenticated approval exchange, and a device enrolled with only a passkey or the recovery key treats every verified pin as seen until another of the user's devices confirms the head or the user re-verifies the contact. The device approval grant ("OZG") gains version 2, which appends the approver's contacts head (u8 flags: head present, confirmed; u32 sequence and 32-byte entry hash), signed with the grant; version 1 grants from older builds still decode and approve, and a grant without a head keeps version 1 bytes (`packages/crypto/README.md`). The approved device pins the head (`contactPins.ts`: the list pin records whether it descends from a trusted start, and `EMPTY_CONTACTS_SEQUENCE` marks a list trusted while empty), so `acceptList` refuses an older signed list; setting up or replacing an identity pins its empty list as confirmed, a server-shown list on its own never confirms a pin, an approver's confirmed head replaces an unconfirmed pin, and list pins stored before this read as confirmed. On an unconfirmed device `loadContacts` and `inspectContact` report verified contacts as seen (`ContactsBook.listConfirmed`), so no project key is wrapped to them, until a comparison made on that browser (`ContactPin.verifiedHere`) or another device confirms: an already certified device can ask for approval again, which the approver completes as certified with no recovery key or sealed account key, and Settings › Encryption › Contacts offers this as "Confirm from another device". No migration and no flag change. Evidence: `packages/crypto/src/devices.test.ts` (version 2 round trips, signed head, version 1 still read, tampering, relabelling and unknown flags refused), `packages/crypto/browser/scenarios.ts`, `apps/web/src/lib/e2ee/contactPins.test.ts`, `apps/web/src/components/EncryptionContacts.test.tsx` and `test/e2ee-contacts.test.ts` (real Worker and SQLite: an approved device shown an older signed list refuses it; a recovery-key device shows a contact verified elsewhere as seen with no key to wrap to until the laptop confirms through the approval exchange; re-verifying on that device counts; an older build's version 1 grant still approves and leaves the list unconfirmed; another browser with no pin now sees the verified contact as seen).
