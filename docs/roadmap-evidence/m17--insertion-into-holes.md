Slice 2, insertion into M01 holes (schema v77, up from v75 (D02 #1650), with v76 claimed by D03 #1651; M17 first claimed v71 and then v74, but clients up to v75 have no `hole`; no kernel change). A standard part may store `hole: { featureId }`, naming an M01 hole earlier in history. It then stands in every bore that hole drills, one solid per bore. Each bore is drilled again, untapped, into the hole's input body and read back as its cylinder. The part sits on the seat (a counterbore's floor, else the entry face; a countersunk head in a countersink sits flush with the entry face) along the bore, and follows the hole when the hole moves. The size is matched (`standardPartForHole`): a screw takes the hole's own ISO or ANSI size, whether clearance or tap drill, and a dowel pin takes the size whose diameter is the hole's. A part with no stored length takes the preferred length matched to its bore (`matchedStandardLengthMm`). Through a clearance hole that is the shortest length that passes the material, a standard nut and two pitches. In a tap drill or blind hole it is the longest length short of the bore's end and of its thread. A dowel pin takes the longest length that fills a blind hole, or the shortest that passes a through hole. A countersunk head's height counts in its length. A hole resized or re-standardized under its part refuses by name ("No ISO dowel pin is 7 mm across."), and so does a part the hole does not take ("Hole 1 takes an ISO M6, not M8."). The hole and its kernel reading share one fit rule (`standardHoleFit`). The dependency graph makes the part a dependent of its hole, so deleting or editing the hole shows it. A content gate refuses `hole` on a document that declares v76 or older. UI: the Standard part tool's Place option offers Into a hole for a screw or dowel pin. A click on a hole's bore sizes the part from the hole and stands its length arrow at the matched length; dragging the arrow stores a length of its own. Evidence: `test/standard-parts-holes.test.ts` (matching rules for clearance, tapped, blind and through holes, dowel pins and countersunk heads; the v77 gate and refusals; on the exact kernel, an M6 screw at its matched 20 mm in a clearance hole, to the volume of the same screw placed by hand; a counterbore seat; a blind tapped hole's 10 mm and the screw following the hole when it moves; one screw in each of a hole's two sketch-point bores, at twice one's volume; a dowel pin filling a blind hole and refusing when the hole is resized), `apps/web/src/lib/inPlace/standardPart.test.ts`, and `test/e2e/standard-parts-holes.spec.ts`. That spec drills an ISO M6 clearance hole with the Hole tool, picks its bore with Standard part › Into a hole, checks the matched 30 mm arrow (20 through, a nut and two pitches), drags it longer, and checks the stored part, its hole and its History row.
