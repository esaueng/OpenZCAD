Gear edge names (after partial-span teeth, PR #1389; no schema change, no kernel change): every gear body now names its edges as well as its faces, so a fillet or chamfer on a gear edge follows later gear edits instead of being pinned to an edge hash. An edge is named from the two faces it lies between, `gear.edge.between.<face>|<face>` with the two face names in string order (`gear.edge.between.gear.face.cap.end|gear.face.collar.end`, `…gear.face.bore|gear.face.cap.start`, `…gear.face.collar.start|gear.face.shoulder.start`), the pair read from the built solid's own topology and never from position or output order; it applies to new spur, helical, herringbone, bevel, worm and enveloping gears and to teeth cut on a body, whose carried faces count by their carried names (`packages/kernel-adapter/src/exact-gear-edges.ts`, [ADR-013](docs/adrs/ADR-013-persistent-topology-lineage.md) M14 amendment). Tooth edges are named the same way rather than left out, so they follow the tooth index of their faces (`…tooth[3].flank.plus|…tooth[3].tip` is tooth 3's edge at any tooth count that has one): the rule is one rule and costs nothing extra. Where one pair of faces shares several edges each is `….piece.<i>.of.<n>`, ordered along the gear's axis, the count part of the name; pieces at one height publish no name, because around the axis no origin survives every edit (the x direction is a parameter and the blank can be turned), so an angular count could hand one piece's name to its neighbour. The one case found is a collar's seam cutting the smooth arc it shares with a flush keep-diameter tooth tip in two (four unnamed half-arcs on a two-collar gear, edges no blend applies to). Edges with an unsupported witness and seams publish no name either. Face hashes and edge hashes are unchanged everywhere, and face names are unchanged but for the one rename below: compared against main for a bored spur, helical, herringbone, bevel, worm and worm-wheel gear and for whole-face and partial teeth of both fits and anchors on a boss, every face name, face hash and edge hash is identical; only edge references are added (482 of a bored 20-tooth spur's 483 edges, the other its bore seam). A neighbouring face is no longer taken for a cap (the one face rename): partial-span teeth named any plane at the picked face's end `gear.face.cap.start|end`, including a face the cylinder runs into (a flange it ends on), which whole-face teeth leave under the name the body's lineage carries, so a datum on that face, and a fillet on its untouched outer rim, lost their reference when the teeth went from the whole face to part of it. Now only the picked face's own cap, the plane that closes the cylinder and looks away from it along the axis, takes `gear.face.cap.*` (and `.body` where teeth reach it); a plane that looks back along the face keeps its carried name at every span, and teeth that run up to it end at an ordinary `gear.face.shoulder.*` in its plane, the neighbour beside it keeping its name by its exact unchanged witness (the only named face of the body on that plane, exactly one result face with that witness), the name the carrier rule gives the unified face under whole-face teeth. On a Ø22 shaft with a Ø26 flange, for teeth below or above the flange at five spans each, face and edge hashes are identical to before and the only names that change are that flange face (to its carried name), the shoulder beside it and the edges named from them; on a plain boss nothing changes. A document saved since #1389 with a reference to such a neighbour under the name `gear.face.cap.*` refuses by name and needs a re-pick. Resolution is unchanged and stays strict: a stored edge name the body no longer publishes refuses as `reference_missing`, never rebinding (teeth run up to an end remove its collar and that rim; fewer teeth remove the higher tooth indices), and a hash-only edge pick stored on a gear before this takes the existing all-or-nothing upgrade to its name on the next build. Kernel limits, not naming ones, recorded as found: it refuses a chamfer on a shoulder's rim and at a split shoulder's tooth space ("could not be chamfered on this body"), refuses both blends on a cap's edge at one tooth and on a narrow (1.1 wide) shoulder's rim, and builds a tooth-tip fillet on some teeth and spans only, so those cases are fillets and the tooth-tip case asserts that the edge is found (built, or the kernel's own geometric refusal, never a lost reference). M14 stays Partial. Both listed items (the meshing-pair helper, PR #1373, and partial-span teeth, PR #1389) have landed and gear edges are named, but "later fillets and holes survive edits" does not yet hold for every reference: (1) the body's part of the picked face's own split end cap, `gear.face.cap.start|end.body`, exists only while partial teeth reach that end, so a reference to it, and to the one edge beside it (its seam with the toothed ring), refuses by name once the caps rejoin (teeth along the whole face, or stopping short of that end); following it into the rejoined cap needs rejoin provenance, unchanged from #1389; (2) where partial teeth run up to a neighbouring face, the edges at which the teeth meet that plane are named from the shoulder there, and under whole-face teeth, which unify the shoulder into the neighbour, from the neighbour, so a reference to one of those edges does not cross between the whole face and teeth that reach the neighbour (the neighbour face and its other edges do); (3) the smooth half-arcs a collar seam cuts, and seams, carry no name, so a reference to one stays hash-only. The row can close when (1) and (2) are followed or the owner narrows the criterion.
