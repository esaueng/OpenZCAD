Split plane through two edges (with R01, slice 1b; owner decision D2, a fixed numeric plane): with the body picked, Split takes two straight edges, on the split body or another, in place of a face or a principal or datum plane. The pair resolves once at pick time (`edgePairPlane` in `packages/geometry`) into Split's existing numeric plane, so there is no new plane kind, no link to the edges and no schema change, and editing the edges later does not move the split. The plane is fitted to the four ends (through their centroid, normal the scatter matrix's least-spread eigenvector), so a rigid motion of the edges never changes the outcome, and the edges are taken in a canonical order only so either pick order and edge direction give a bit-identical plane. Its origin is the centroid; its normal's largest component is positive, and a negative Offset slides the plane the other way. Coplanar crossing or parallel pairs resolve; pairs whose four ends all lie within the linear tolerance of one line (`collinear`), pairs with an end farther than the linear tolerance from the fitted plane (`skew`, the three-point plane's point-to-plane rule, so crossing lines up to twice the tolerance apart count as meeting) and curved edges (`curved-edge`) are refused before any rebuild with their own sentences after "Plane:", with the edges kept to re-pick. Oracles: the 20 mm block halved 4000/4000 by x + z = 20, both through the R01 three-point datum on the same edges' vertices and through the picked pair's stored numbers; slid 5√2 along the normal, 1000 on the normal's side and 7000 opposite; deepened to 30 after the split, still x + z = 20 at 8000/4000. Tolerances: collinear and skew at and just past the linear tolerance; the review and re-check repros, a 4000-pair permutation property test and a 2000-case oracle test (coplanar, nearly parallel, skew and collinear pairs built in random rigid poses at 1e-3 to 1e5 mm) all pass.
