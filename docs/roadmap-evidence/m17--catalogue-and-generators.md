Slice 1, catalogue and generators (schema v69, up from v67 on main, with v68 claimed by D02 #1618; no kernel change, Remus 2026.1.141). The `standard-part` feature kind generates a new body from its standard's table (`packages/shared/src/standard-parts.ts`): ISO 4762 / ASME B18.3 socket head cap screws, ISO 4017 / ASME B18.2.1 hex head screws, ISO 10642 / ASME B18.3 countersunk socket screws, ISO 4032 / ASME B18.2.2 and B18.6.3 hex nuts, ISO 7089 / ASME B18.22.1 plain washers, heat-set threaded inserts (typical sizes, labelled so: no standard fixes their outside), ISO 8734 / ASME B18.8.2 dowel pins and ISO 15 / ABMA deep groove ball bearings. Every blank is an exact revolved profile, a chamfered hex prism or a torus-grooved ring; screws, nuts and inserts are threaded by the M15 thread generator through `cutResolvedThread` (ISO 68-1, also the Unified basic profile), and an insert's knurl is herringbone teeth cut by the M14 gear generator (`cutGearTeethOnFace`), keeping its outside diameter. Nothing is imported or meshed. Approximations, labelled in the catalogue: a flat-bottomed hex socket, an ANSI key depth of half the head, dowel chamfers of a quarter radius, and bearing balls of 0.3 (D − d) on the pitch circle in raceways 4% wider; the boundary dimensions are the standards'. A screw is threaded from its tip for the standard's thread length and stops at least two pitches short of its head with a runout. Faces are named by role (`standard-part.face.seat`, `…head.top`, `…tip`, `…bore`, `…outside`) where one face holds it, and thread faces by the thread generator's roles. The Standard part tool (Create group) places a part where a flat face or plane is clicked: a screw, insert or dowel pin goes into the face, a nut, washer or bearing sits on it; Options choose kind, head, system and size; a screw's or dowel pin's length is an arrow along its axis; History reads the designation ("ISO 4762 M6×1 × 20 socket head cap screw") and reopens the part as stored. A content gate refuses the kind on a document that declares v68 or older. Evidence: `test/standard-parts.test.ts` (catalogue consistency across every row; designations; refusals; the v69 gate; command validation, undo, redo, edit and replay; a washer and a dowel pin to their closed-form volumes; an M6 × 20 socket screw to its head and length with its thread inside the major-to-minor band; placement along a downward axis; hex, countersunk and nut dimensions; a knurled, tapped M3 insert; a 608 bearing between its ring and ball bounds; ANSI parts in an inch document; every table's smallest and largest threaded size and every unthreaded row built without a warning), `apps/web/src/lib/inPlace/standardPart.test.ts`, and `test/e2e/standard-parts.spec.ts`, which clicks the block's top face, chooses an ISO M6 socket head screw and drags its length arrow from 16 to about 24 mm, then checks the stored part and its History row. Measured on this machine, one threaded part costs one to three seconds of exact rebuild, mostly display tessellation of its helical faces at the 0.02 % chord; the simplified thread display (slice 4) addresses many fasteners.
