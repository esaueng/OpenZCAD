Privacy policy

Effective October 26, 2026

In short

  • You can model without an account. Your projects then stay in your browser and we do not receive them, unless you use the AI assistant, which sends your request to us and to an outside AI provider.
  • If you sign in, we store your email address and the projects you save to your account.
  • Cloud projects are stored in readable form unless they are end-to-end encrypted. End-to-end encryption is opt-in per account during the beta and may not yet be available to your account. Even for encrypted projects, the server sees metadata such as sizes, timestamps, who the members are and which device saved each version.
  • There is no way for us to recover encryption keys. If you lose every device and your recovery key, your encrypted cloud projects are lost; copies on your devices remain.
  • Optional usage analytics are off until you turn them on. We do not sell your data and show no advertising.
  • You can delete your cloud projects, your profile, or both, from Settings.

1. Who we are

OpenZCAD is a browser-based CAD application. This policy covers the hosted OpenZCAD service at zcad.app and the OpenZCAD desktop app for macOS when it is connected to that service. The service is operated by Esau Engineering LLC (“we”, “us”). Contact us by email at help@zcad.app.

This policy is governed by the laws of the State of Michigan, United States. It does not cover OpenZCAD instances that other people run; see Self-hosted deployments.

2. Using OpenZCAD without an account

Modeling, imports, exports, drawings and thumbnails are computed on your device. Without an account, your projects, their history, imported files, settings and assistant conversations are kept in your browser’s storage (IndexedDB and local storage), and we do not receive them. Anyone with access to your device or browser profile can read that data.

The app also keeps a small local log of recent direct edits for diagnostics, which can include a copy of the project being edited. It holds the newest 40 entries, is never sent automatically, and is removed when you clear the site’s data in your browser.

Cloud features switch. Settings → Account → Cloud features turns this device’s sign-in, sync, sharing, assistant and optional analytics on or off. It is on by default. Your browser still downloads the app itself from our servers either way.

Project files. Export project writes an .openzcad backup file to your device. We do not receive it, and it is not encrypted.

3. Your account

You sign in with an email address and a one-time code; there are no passwords. For your account we store:

  • Your email address. Your account ID is derived from it, and your display name is the part before the “@”.
  • Sign-in codes. A six-digit code we email you. We store only a keyed hash of it, with the number of attempts. It works once and expires after 10 minutes.
  • Sessions. A random session token in a cookie; we store only a hash of it. A session lasts 30 days unless you sign out first.
  • Abuse protection. The sign-in form uses Cloudflare Turnstile, and we pass your IP address to that check. Sign-in rate limits are counted under keyed hashes of your email and IP address, not the raw values.
  • Synced settings, so your preferences follow you between devices.
  • A personal AI key, if you add one. We store it encrypted under a key held by the server, with a short hint so you can recognise it.

We use this data to sign you in, keep you signed in, protect the service from abuse, and provide the features you use.

4. Projects in the cloud

When you are signed in with cloud features on, projects you create are saved to your account and kept up to date by cloud autosave (Settings → Saving). A project you made while signed out stays on that device until you choose to add it to your account. Cloud data is held on Cloudflare: records in a D1 database, files in R2 object storage, and live collaboration state in Durable Objects. Connections use HTTPS.

A cloud project is stored in one of two ways.

Standard projects (readable by the server)

Unless a project is end-to-end encrypted, its document, name, parameters, sketches and text, feature history, saved revisions, imported files, meshes, thumbnails, measurements and workspace view state are stored in readable form. Cloudflare encrypts stored data at rest, which protects against lost disks but not against us, anyone who gains control of our Cloudflare account or server code, Cloudflare staff with access, or legal demands. The server reads these projects to check and convert them, merge live edits, serve share links and name the project in invitation emails.

End-to-end encrypted projects

An encrypted project is encrypted on your device before it is uploaded, and only devices holding its keys can read it. See the next section for what that does and does not hide.

We also store, for every project, its sharing state (members, roles, invitations, share links and a history of sharing changes), its place on your shelf (pinned, archived, in the Trash, order) and the sizes and counts used for storage quotas.

5. End-to-end encrypted projects

End-to-end encryption is opt-in per account during the beta, and it is being made available gradually, so your account may not have it yet; Settings → Encryption appears when it does. Once you turn on Encrypt new projects, the server accepts only encrypted new projects from your account. Projects you already have stay as they are until you choose to encrypt them; when you do, your browser encrypts the project, its save states and its files, and the readable copy is then deleted from your account (see backups).

What the server cannot read

For an encrypted project, the server stores only encrypted data for the project document and every saved version, its name, save-state names and reasons, imported files and meshes, thumbnails, measurements, workspace view state, archived exports and share-link snapshots. Invitation emails for encrypted projects do not name the project.

What the server still sees

  • account email addresses and display names;
  • which accounts are members of a project, their roles, and invitations;
  • how many projects and versions you have, the size of each stored item, timestamps, version numbers and how often you save;
  • which account and which device signed each saved version, and which encryption key generation it uses;
  • presence (active or idle) during live editing;
  • your device list: device labels, their public keys, and when devices and unlock methods were added or removed;
  • your contacts list: whose keys you have verified, and when;
  • the format fields of each encrypted item (its kind, size, format and schema version), your shelf state, and whether you allowed the assistant on a project.

What encryption does not protect against

  • Altered application code. On the web, our server delivers the app’s code, so a compromised deployment could ship code that reads your keys. The signed desktop app is the stronger client.
  • The AI assistant, which must send what it reads to an AI provider in readable form; it asks first (see The AI assistant).
  • Copies a member made before being removed; removal protects only later versions.
  • A compromised device, or the project copies kept in your browser, which are as safe as your device.
  • Deletion or withholding of data by someone with access to storage. Encryption makes tampering detectable, not impossible.

6. Keys and recovery

Your encryption keys are created on your devices. Setting up encryption gives you a recovery key, and you can add passkeys and approve further devices from one you already use. In a browser, a device’s private keys are stored as non-extractable keys in IndexedDB; in the desktop app they are kept in the macOS Keychain. The server stores your public keys, your signed device list, and copies of your keys that are themselves encrypted to your devices, recovery key or passkeys, which it cannot open.

There is no server-held recovery key. We cannot recover your keys or read your encrypted projects, and support cannot restore them. If you lose every device and your recovery key, your encrypted cloud projects cannot be recovered by you or by us. Copies on your devices and any .openzcad backups you exported remain.

7. Sharing and share links

  • Members you invite can see the project, its history and who else belongs to it. An invitation email shows the person you invite your email address and, for a standard project, the project name. Invitations expire after 7 days.
  • Standard share links let anyone who has the link open the project without an account, for 30 days after the link is created or until you revoke it.
  • Encrypted share links carry the key to a snapshot of the project in the part of the link after “#”, which browsers never send to a server. The server keeps only a hash of the link’s access token and the encrypted snapshot, so it cannot read the shared project. Anyone who has the full link can. Visitors’ browsers trust a link only while its owner has confirmed sharing within the last 30 days, and you can revoke it at any time.

8. Live collaboration

An open cloud project sends edits to a collaboration room on our server, even when nobody else is present, unless you turn off Settings → Account → Project sharing. Members see when you are active or idle. For a standard project, the room keeps the latest document and up to 20 earlier snapshots, in readable form, for the life of the project. For an encrypted project, the room passes on and stores only encrypted versions.

9. The AI assistant

The assistant runs only when you send it a request, and needs cloud features on. The built-in assistant also works while you are signed out; for those requests we derive a pseudonym from your IP address with a secret key, and use it, not the address, for usage limits.

What is sent. Each request carries:

  • your prompt and up to 12 earlier turns of the conversation;
  • the project’s ID, name, units and version, and a summary of the model: every parameter, every feature’s full definition including sketches and any text in them, each body’s name, volume, bounding box and measured faces and edges, the dimensions the app found editable, and warnings;
  • your current selection;
  • any images or drawing pages you attach, with their file names;
  • the list of edits the app can apply, and whether the request is an automatic retry.

Our server reads each request to pass it on, and adds its own instructions, any custom instructions from your settings, and a pseudonymous identifier derived from your account (or from the signed-out pseudonym) with a secret key, not your email address or IP address. Imported STEP files and meshes are not sent.

Who receives it. The built-in assistant sends requests to the AI provider our deployment is configured with. Today that is OpenRouter, which passes them to the provider of the selected model; the provider or model can change, and Settings → AI Assistant shows which one applies, with a link to its data policy. The AI provider may keep this data, or use it for training and service improvement, under its own policies. Do not send sensitive or confidential designs. If you add your own API key, requests go through our server to the provider and model you chose, under that provider’s terms.

Encrypted projects. On an end-to-end encrypted project, the assistant sends nothing until you allow it for that project. It first shows you exactly what each request contains and which provider and model it goes to, and asks again if that changes. The choice is yours alone and per project; you can stop it from the assistant, the project’s properties or Settings → Encryption.

What we keep. We do not store your prompts, conversations or the assistant’s replies on the server; your conversation history stays in your browser. We keep request and cost counters for usage limits. What has been sent to a provider cannot be recalled.

10. Analytics

Analytics go to Glimpse, our own analytics service on Cloudflare, which keeps only aggregate counters, with no record per person. None of the levels below sends model contents, geometry, file or project names, email addresses, IP addresses, URLs, error text, or account or device identifiers.

  • Basic statistics (always on for the hosted service): the server counts API requests, server errors and response times in fixed ranges.
  • Optional usage analytics (off unless you turn them on in Settings → Privacy & data): fixed event names with fixed value ranges, such as which kind of feature was used and size and timing ranges. The choice applies to that browser only. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control turn them off, the desktop app never sends them, and turning them off stops sending immediately.
  • Cloud account statistics (off unless you allow them): your account’s project, revision and storage-size counts are included in hourly service-wide totals for 30 days, or until you withdraw. The permission record holds your account ID and its expiry.

Totals already received cannot be traced to you, so they cannot be removed after you withdraw.

11. Cookies and browser storage

The app sets one cookie, __Host-openzcad_session, when you sign in. It keeps you signed in, is sent only to our server over HTTPS, cannot be read by the page’s scripts, and expires after 30 days or when you sign out. We use no advertising or cross-site tracking cookies.

The app’s own code loads no third-party scripts except Cloudflare Turnstile on the sign-in form, which processes information about your browser and network to check that a person is signing in, under Cloudflare’s terms. Our security policy also allows Cloudflare Web Analytics, a cookieless page-view counter that Cloudflare can add to the pages it serves for us.

Everything else the app keeps — local projects and files, settings, your analytics choice, the cloud features switch, a random device ID used to sync your workspace, assistant conversations and, if you set up encryption, your device keys — stays in your browser’s storage unless a feature described here sends part of it.

12. Emails we send

We send sign-in codes to your address, and invitation emails to the people you invite. We send no marketing email. Emails are sent through Cloudflare’s email service.

15. Deleting data and your rights

Signed in, open Settings → Privacy & data → Delete cloud data. Each choice asks you to confirm.

  • Delete all cloud projects: every project you own, with its revisions, files and collaboration room. Your profile stays.
  • Delete cloud profile: your email address, synced settings, personal AI key, sessions, invitations sent to your address, and your encryption key records (public keys, device list and encrypted key copies). Your projects stay in the cloud under your account ID, with no email address attached.
  • Delete all cloud data: your profile and every project you own. Collaborators lose access to your projects, and your memberships, invitations and share links are removed.

Deleting cloud data does not touch projects stored in your browser; delete those from the Trash on the start screen, or clear the site’s data. Data already sent to an AI provider and analytics totals cannot be recalled, and backups may keep data for the period in section 14.

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or take a copy of your personal data, to object to or restrict some processing, to withdraw consent, and to complain to a data protection authority. Export project gives you an .openzcad copy of any project at any time. For anything the app does not cover, email help@zcad.app.

16. The macOS desktop app

The desktop app works on projects on your Mac. When you sign in, it connects to the hosted service at zcad.app, and this policy applies. You approve the sign-in in your browser; the app keeps its sign-in credential and, if you set up encryption, its device keys in the macOS Keychain, and the app’s web view never receives the private key bytes. The desktop app never sends optional usage analytics.

17. Security

We use HTTPS, store sign-in codes and session tokens only as hashes, encrypt personal AI keys, rate-limit sign-in and the API, and check that each request may reach the project it names. No system is perfectly secure, and standard projects are readable by anyone who controls the hosting account. If a breach affects your data, we will tell you as the law requires.

18. Self-hosted deployments

OpenZCAD is open source, and anyone can run their own instance. Its operator controls its data, chooses its AI provider and sets its retention. This policy does not apply to self-hosted instances, and we receive no data from them.

19. International transfers

Your data may be processed in the United States and in other countries where Cloudflare and the AI provider operate.

20. Children

OpenZCAD is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their personal data.
